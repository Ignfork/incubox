01 · the argument

Crypto gave you open. Apps gave you easy.

For years you had to choose. Self-custody and public settlement on one side, one-tap simplicity on the other. Solana is the first place we've seen both hold at once.

open

Open

People hold their own keys. Records settle in public where anyone can check them. Nothing depends on trusting the builder.

easy

Easy

One tap, no seed-phrase lecture, no waiting. The thing works the way normal apps work, and fees are small enough to forget.

both

Both

A fast, parallel L1 that pays fees in SOL. INCUBOX builds for that middle ground, and tells you when a chain isn't needed at all.

07 · docs

Before you open the chamber.

Quickstart, first prompt, the plan, and the limits.

What do I give it?

One sentence about who it's for and what they do. "A tip jar for my stream" is enough. The agent asks for the rest in its open questions.

What does the plan look like?

A short written plan, a list of every feature it considered marked keep or cut with a reason, and the questions it couldn't answer for you. You edit the plan; the build follows it.

Will it always put things on-chain?

No. Programs are used only where state has to be public. If nothing needs to be, the plan says "no chain needed" and you get a normal web app.

Who holds the keys?

You do. The agent deploys to devnet on its own, then prepares mainnet for you to sign from your wallet. It never takes custody of keys or funds.

What is $INCUBOX for?

$INCUBOX is the project's token. You don't need it to scope, build, ship or stay. Treat it as high risk.

What are the limits?

Consumer-sized products: pages, small programs, simple flows. It won't build exchanges, bridges or anything that holds other people's money without a human audit.
